Concept 1 · The Basics

🎯 What is Probability?

Probability measures how likely something is to happen — always a number between 0 (impossible) and 1 (certain).

P(Event) = Favorable Outcomes ÷ Total Possible Outcomes
Example

🎲 Rolling a 3 on a six-sided die:

P(3) = 1 ÷ 6 ≈ 0.167 (about 17%)

💡 Probabilities can be written as fractions, decimals, or percentages. They all say the same thing!
Concept 2 · The Opposite

🔄 Complement Rule

The probability that something does NOT happen equals 1 minus the probability it does happen.

P(not A) = 1 − P(A)
Example

🌦️ Chance of rain is 30% → P(no rain) = 1 − 0.3 = 0.7 (70%)

💡 P(A) + P(not A) always = 1. Together they cover every possibility!
Concept 3 · OR Rules

🤝 Mutually Exclusive

Events that can't both happen at the same time (like rolling a 2 or a 5).

P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B)

🎲 P(2 or 5) = 1/6 + 1/6 = 2/6 = 1/3

🔀 Can Overlap

Events that can happen together (like a card that is red and a face card).

P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B)

🃏 P(red or face) = 26/52 + 12/52 − 6/52 = 32/52 ≈ 62%
Subtract the overlap so you don't count it twice!

Concept 4 · AND Rules

🔗 Independent Events

One event doesn't affect the other (like flipping a coin twice).

P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B)

🪙 P(H and H) = 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25

⛓️ Dependent Events

The first event changes the odds for the second (like drawing cards without putting them back).

P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B|A)

🃏 Two aces in a row: 4/52 × 3/51 = 1/221
P(B|A) means "B, given A already happened."

Concept 5 · Given That…

🔍 Conditional Probability

What's the probability of A, knowing B already happened? We narrow our sample space to just the outcomes where B is true.

P(A | B) = P(A and B) ÷ P(B)
Example

🃏 P(King | face card) = P(King and face card) ÷ P(face card)

= (4/52) ÷ (12/52) = 4/12 = 1/3 ≈ 33%

💡 The bar | just means "given that." Read P(A|B) as "probability of A given B."
Concept 6 · Flip It!

🔁 Bayes' Theorem

Lets you reverse a conditional probability. If you know P(B|A), you can find P(A|B).

P(A | B) = [P(B | A) × P(A)] ÷ P(B)
Worked example · medical test 🧪

1% of people have a disease. The test catches 90% of sick people, but also flags 9% of healthy people. You test positive. What's the chance you're actually sick?

  1. P(sick and positive) = 0.01 × 0.90 = 0.009
  2. P(healthy and positive) = 0.99 × 0.09 = 0.0891
  3. P(positive) = 0.009 + 0.0891 = 0.0981
  4. P(sick | positive) = 0.009 ÷ 0.0981 ≈ 9%
🧠 Surprising, right? Most positives come from the huge healthy group. Ask: what do I know vs. what do I want — then plug in.

📋 Formula Cheat Sheet

Basic Probability
P(A) = favorable ÷ total
Complement
P(not A) = 1 − P(A)
OR (exclusive)
P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B)
OR (overlapping)
P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B)
AND (independent)
P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B)
AND (dependent)
P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B|A)
Conditional
P(A|B) = P(A and B) ÷ P(B)
Bayes' Theorem
P(A|B) = P(B|A) × P(A) ÷ P(B)

Textbook symbols decoder

  • A ∩ B = "A and B"
  • A ∪ B = "A or B"
  • A | B = "A given B"
  • A′ or Aᶜ = "not A"
