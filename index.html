Probability 🎲

Short chunks. Bold formulas. Real examples. Zero fluff.

Concept 1 · The Basics

🎯 What is Probability?

Probability measures how likely something is to happen — always a number between 0 (impossible) and 1 (certain).

P(Event) = Favorable Outcomes ÷ Total Possible Outcomes
Example

🎲 Rolling a 3 on a six-sided die:

P(3) = 1 ÷ 6 ≈ 0.167 (about 17%)

💡 Probabilities can be written as fractions, decimals, or percentages. They all say the same thing!
Concept 2 · The Opposite

🔄 Complement Rule

The probability that something does NOT happen equals 1 minus the probability it does happen.

P(not A) = 1 − P(A)
Example

🌦️ Chance of rain is 30% → P(no rain) = 1 − 0.3 = 0.7 (70%)

💡 P(A) + P(not A) always = 1. They must cover every possibility!
Concept 3 · OR Rules

🤝 Mutually Exclusive

Events that can't both happen at the same time (like rolling a 2 or a 5).

P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B)

🎲 P(2 or 5) = 1/6 + 1/6 = 2/6

🔀 Can Overlap

Events that can happen together (like drawing a red card or a face card).

P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B)

Subtract overlap so you don't count it twice! 🃏

Concept 4 · AND Rules

🔗 Independent Events

One event doesn't affect the other (like flipping a coin twice).

P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B)

🪙 P(H and H) = 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25

🔗 Dependent Events

First event changes the odds for the second (like drawing cards without replacing).

P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B|A)

P(B|A) means "B given A already happened" 🃏

Concept 5 · Given That...

🔍 Conditional Probability

What's the probability of A, knowing B already happened? We narrow our sample space to just the outcomes where B is true.

P(A | B) = P(A and B) ÷ P(B)
Example

🃏 In a deck, P(King | face card) = P(King and face card) ÷ P(face card)

= (4/52) ÷ (12/52) = 4/12 = 1/3 ≈ 33%

💡 The vertical bar | just means "given that." Read P(A|B) as "probability of A given B."
Concept 6 · Flip It!

🔁 Bayes' Theorem

Lets you reverse a conditional probability. If you know P(B|A), you can find P(A|B).

P(A | B) = [P(B | A) × P(A)] ÷ P(B)
Memory Hook

🧪 A medical test is positive. What's the chance you actually have the disease?
Bayes lets you flip "chance of positive test given disease" → "chance of disease given positive test."

🧠 Bayes is powerful but tricky. Focus on what you know vs what you want to find — then plug in!
